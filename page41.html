Theme – Definition

NARROW‑MINDEDNESS

The quality or state of being unwilling or unready to accept ideas, beliefs, or behaviors that are different from your own.

IMAGINATION

The faculty or action of forming new ideas, or mental images or concepts of external objects not present to the senses.

Global Issue – Definition & Impact

RIGID SYSTEMS & SUPPRESSION

Rigid educational systems, suppression of creative thinking, and ideological extremism refer to situations where people are forced to follow strict rules, accept certain beliefs, and avoid questioning traditional ideas.

These situations discourage creativity, curiosity, and independent thinking.

Analysis

In The Little Prince, adults often see only the surface — a hat instead of a boa constrictor digesting an elephant. This is narrow‑mindedness: refusing to look beyond what is familiar or practical.

The Little Prince and the narrator (as a child) use imagination to see deeper meaning — like the sheep inside the crate. This conflict mirrors real‑world problems.

Rigid educational systems, suppression of creative thinking, and ideological extremism force people to accept one way of thinking and reject new ideas. When this happens, imagination is treated as a waste of time instead of a vital human ability.

Result

When imagination is suppressed, people become passive, conformist, and unable to question unfair rules. They stop seeing possibilities and accepting differences.

In the story, the narrator gives up drawing after adults criticize his boa constrictor picture. This shows the cost of narrow‑mindedness: a creative soul is silenced.

In real life, rigid education produces students who can memorize facts but cannot think critically. Ideological extremism leads to intolerance and conflict. The result is a society that values obedience over curiosity, conformity over creativity, and fear over understanding.

Conclusion

The Little Prince reminds us that “what is essential is invisible to the eye.” Imagination and open‑mindedness are not childish extras — they are essential for understanding ourselves, connecting with others, and solving problems.

To fight narrow‑mindedness, we must encourage curiosity, creative expression, and independent thinking at home, in school, and in society.

By protecting the child within each person, we build a more compassionate, innovative, and just world.
